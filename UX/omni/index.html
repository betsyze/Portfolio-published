TLDR: the current process was leaving revenue at risk, and rendering opportunities for improvement invisible.

Context & problem

Role: UX Co-Lead | Timeline: February - June 2026

As a trailing telecom provider, Cox was under mounting pressure to improve customer acquisition and retention ahead of a company acquisition. In the new organization, customer experience maturity would directly impact valuation.

We pushed hard on a rapid timeline to create a new integrated enterprise product. The product, nicknamed "Omni Intelligence", would help internal teams discover and describe user data insights faster and more efficiently, which in turn would inform technical teams on when and how to ship vital site upgrades.

The Omni tool

In conversations with their Analytics team, we learned Cox collects a wealth of data that at project launch had been gathering dust. That data = real customer conversations with the Oliver self service chat bot, and their chat and call transcripts with live agents.

While Cox possessed a wealth of customer data, it lived across disconnected systems, making it impossible for Customer Experience (CX) and User Experience teams to work together and see how experiences unfold across channels, or how current behaviors reveal underlying frustration points. Identifying, watching, and moderating these patterns has been vital in driving better outcomes for Cox's declining customer satisfaction scores.

Reporting was largely ad hoc and trend-based, masking recurring frustrations and crisis points within customer journeys. Without a way to connect intentions, actions, and their outcomes on the part of both users and service agents, Cox couldn’t identify what was driving churn, self-service failure, or unnecessary agent costs.

Where I came in

My role: UX designer turned design engineer.

I'd been building design systems and web experiences for some time. At home, I'd been teaching myself the foundations of code through HTML/CSS, Git & Github, and React. I was looking for an opportunity to work with backend engineers, and extend my front-end expertise to build a net-new experience.

It was the perfect opportunity to work on a a project that combined frontier AI capabilities with interaction design.

Impact

The tool we shipped was able to be scaled into Cox's process just before the client was acquired by Charter Communications. The tool was effective as proof of value for team members, and contributing to their remaining on staff. We helped Cox’s UX team quantify the proof of their value, and position them as AI native builders.

Initial breakdown

Our insight

If Cox could stitch together end‑to‑end customer journeys—layered with turn‑level behavioral labels and outcome data—they could rank the specific journey sequences most likely to drive churn, resolution, or conversion. This would enable the business to focus enhancements where they mattered most: increasing digital self‑service, reducing operational overhead, and improving retention.

Users reported their questions and frustrations regularly in their own words to the Oliver chatbot tool. But: there wasn't a system in place to transform these frustrations into insights for Cox's site architects.

With AI advancements, we saw an opportunity to take these insights, grounded in real customer language, and wield them in a way that would accelerate research and decision making time at scale.

Our strategy: make an integrated dashboard

Extend OMNI’s intent infrastructure to unlock journey‑level behavior analytics.

Cox collects so much information already: now, the goal should be to synthesize and organize that data. The builders behind the website need to know what customers did, why they did it, and what they’re likely to do next.

Solution

Omni Intelligence: Journey Analytics was designed to transform fragmented customer data into a unified, actionable view of the end‑to‑end experience. By connecting digital, agent, and virtual assistant touchpoints, the platform mapped complete customer journeys and applied AI-driven intent and behavior labeling at each turn.

This approach allowed Cox to identify high‑impact journey patterns tied to key outcomes such as churn, containment, and resolution. Journey rankings highlighted where self‑service broke down, where agents needed better enablement, and where virtual agent conversational design could be improved.

The end goal was to empower teams to make data-backed decisions grounded in real customer behavior, rather than assumptions or trends.
